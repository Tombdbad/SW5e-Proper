## Rules data: one source (shared/data)
- `npm run import:sw5e` (shared/scripts/import-sw5e.ts) downloads every dataset from `https://sw5eapi.azurewebsites.net/api` and writes `shared/data/sw5e/<name>.json` plus `manifest.json` (data version 199, 36 datasets, 9.2 MB, about 15 seconds). It strips storage metadata and duplicate "...Json" / "...Enum" fields, repairs the API's broken dashes and apostrophes, gives every record an `id`, and stores class, archetype and species features with their owner. `SW5E_CACHE=<dir>` reads saved responses instead of the network.
- It also derives two small files from book text: `travel.json` (the nine galactic regions, the hyperspace travel-time table, realspace times, starship size table) and `lookups.json` (languages, the nine alignments, point-buy costs, wealth by level).
- Two more files are derived from book text (2026-10-15): `transport.json` (the Expanded Content's mounts, tack and vehicles tables and the Player's Handbook travel pace table: speeds, costs, carrying capacity, miles an hour) and `tools.json` (Wretched Hives' Tool Proficiencies chapter: for each tool the skills it helps, its special use and its sample activities with their DCs). `DERIVE_ONLY=1 npm run import:sw5e` rebuilds just those two from the files on disk, without the network.
- Do not edit `shared/data/sw5e/*.json` and do not define rules content anywhere else. Re-run the importer to refresh.
- `shared/data/index.ts`: `loadDataset(name)`, one lazy loader per dataset. `client/src/lib/sw5e/{species,classes,archetypes,backgrounds,feats,forcePowers,techPowers,equipment,items,monsters,npcs,droids,vehicles,starships,maneuvers,fightingStyles,weaponProperties,conditions,skills,exploits,constants}.ts` keep their old export names but only map that data. `client/src/lib/data/*.ts` re-export them.
- Server: `server/sw5eData.ts` loads datasets on demand; `GET /api/sw5e` (manifest) and `GET /api/sw5e/:dataset?search=&fields=&limit=`.
- Bundle: pages load lazily (App.tsx), so the home page script fell from 1.9 MB to 0.4 MB; character creation loads a 2.6 MB chunk (0.5 MB gzipped) with the data it needs.
- The setting's known systems and planets (`client/src/lib/sw5e/starSystems.ts`, `locations.ts`) are not rules data and are not from sw5e.com; the world generator uses them as seeds.

## World generator (shared/logic/index.ts)
- Model: `World { areas, paths, startId, antagonist, partyLevel, ... }`. An `Area` has a kind (galaxy, region, system, planet, settlement, building, room), a parent, a position, a danger level, `gateway` (the way in and out of its parent) and its contents: `occupants`, `ships`, `loot`. A `Path` joins two areas of the same kind: hyperlane, sublight, route, street or door, with a travel time or distance and optional `locked` DC and `hidden`.
- Every area is a place to stand: a room, the street in front of a building, the outskirts of a settlement, a planet's orbit, a system's jump point. `exitsOf` lists the moves from an area (along a path, in through a gateway, out to the parent); `findPath` gives the route between any two areas. Regions only group systems.
- How it builds: regions and hyperspace times from `travel.json`; known systems placed in their regions and generated ones added so no region is empty; hyperlanes as a minimum spanning tree per region plus one lane between neighbouring regions and a few shortcuts; planets on sublight lanes; then for a planet: a starport settlement, a second settlement and one or two wilderness sites suited to the biome, joined by routes; buildings by settlement type joined by streets; rooms by building type with fixed layouts, locked and hidden doors. Only the starting planet is built in full; `expandArea` builds the rest when someone goes there.
- Placement, all from the rules data: staff by what a room is for, with gear picked by name from the equipment data; faction sentries and hostile garrisons from the stat blocks a faction fields; beasts whose text matches the planet's biome; horrors in ruins; utility droids in ports and workshops; fights sized by the 5th edition XP budgets for the party's level and size and the area's danger; stat-block loadouts read from their attacks and armor; ships assembled from starship equipment by size; loot caches by room purpose, with credits sized against the Wealth by Level table and enhanced items of a rarity suited to the level.
- `validateWorld(world, catalog?)` lists what is wrong: wrong nesting, paths between different kinds or parents, a container without a gateway, areas unreachable from the start, and (with the catalog) any stat block or item that is not in the rules data.
- Edit functions keep the map sound and say why when they refuse: `updateArea`, `addArea` (joined to its nearest neighbour), `removeArea` (neighbours are re-joined), `connectAreas`, `disconnectAreas` (refused if it would cut the map), `setGateway`, `setStart`.
- Server (`server/sw5eRoutes.ts`): `POST /api/world/generate | expand | populate | validate | occupant`. The world is saved in the campaign's `galaxyMap` column; `currentLocation` is then an area id.
- UI: the campaign page (`components/Campaign/CampaignManager.tsx`) generates, browses and edits the world and now saves everything it edits. Play (`pages/GameBoard.tsx`) shows the current area, who and what is there, and its exits; enemies bar the way onward until beaten; fights use the stat block's AC, HP, attack and damage and the weapons the character carries; the fallen leave their gear; loot goes to the character; locks and hidden things take ability checks; `Explore` builds an unvisited place. The Play galaxy map draws the world's systems and hyperlanes.
- The same seed and options give the same world.

- Floors (life 4; "Levels" in shared/logic: `levelsOf`, `linkLevels`, `planBuilding`). Rooms have `layout.level`: 0 the ground, below 0 beneath it (cellar, basement, crypt, vault, detention block, hidden compartment, escape tunnel), above 0 over it (apartments, quarters, offices, command and operations rooms, control tower, rooftop, lookout, bridge, port office), and a gallery half a floor over a tall room (main bar, workfloor, storage floor, reading hall, ...). Anything that holds a ship (or is called a docking bay, hangar, berth, arm) is on the building's top floor (at least the first above ground; landing fields and pads are the ground) and has a `roof`: `open` to the sky, or `hangar` (doors that slide back, animated) where the air is hostile (volcanic, ice, barren, gas giant, ocean, stations). Rooms on different floors are not joined directly: a room of its own lies between them (`layout.form`: stairs, or a lift where a ship room is one end, `climb` says which way the floor rises), plus now and then corridors (large buildings), elevated walkways (upper floors of urban, ocean and station buildings), a ladder up the outside wall (a gateway room, a second way in to an upper room) and a gallery with a flight of `stairs` assets either side. Nothing is ever put in those (furnishing skips them; a gallery is furnished). Rooms on other floors may stand over each other (`clash` compares floors as well as squares). The 3D map draws each room at its floor, the party climbs the steps, what is over a room being looked into is taken away and the ground is taken away from a cellar. Worlds before life 4 get all of it once: every built building is planned again (the secret ways the party found stay found).

## Play rules (Phase 2f, 2026-10-13)
Where the code is: `client/src/lib/sw5e/characterCalculation.ts` (features, their effects, levelling), `client/src/lib/sw5e/rules.ts` (casting), `client/src/lib/stores/useCombat.ts` and `components/Combat/PlayCombat.tsx` (the fight), `shared/logic/index.ts` (quests, ships, fuel, passage), `pages/GameBoard.tsx` (Play), `pages/CharacterManagement.tsx` (level-up panel, Features tab).
- Rests: a short rest spends one Hit Die (class die + Constitution; `characters.hit_dice_used`) and restores tech points; a long rest needs 1 hit point, restores hit points and power points and returns half the Hit Dice. No resting with enemies in the area.
- Dying: at 0 hit points the character rolls death saves on their turn (10+ succeeds, a 1 is two failures, a 20 gets up with 1 hit point); damage that carries past the hit point maximum kills outright. Three successes: stable, and the character comes to with 1 hit point (back where they came from, when that is known). Three failures: `conditions: ["Dead"]`.
- Powers: cost is the power's level + 1, at-will powers are free. `powerEffect` reads the power's text: an attack roll, a saving throw (half on a save where the text says so) or healing, with the dice it names; at-will dice grow at 5th, 11th and 17th level. Force powers use Wisdom (light), Charisma (dark) or the better (universal); tech powers Intelligence. Save DC 8 + proficiency + modifier. Of the 465 powers about 120 resolve this way; the rest are cast, paid for and described. Healing powers can be cast between fights. Stat blocks carry their ability modifiers for saves. Creation and level-up require the power a power builds on.
- Features that change the numbers (read from their text, so only where the books use the same words each time): natural armor and Unarmored Defense ("your AC is 12 + your Dexterity modifier"), unarmed strike dice (41 species, and the Monk's Martial Arts die), skill and saving throw proficiencies, hit points per level (Toughness, Durable), speed (Mobile, the Monk's Unarmored Movement), and a feat's +1 to an ability. A species' unarmed strike is saved in the character's `weapons` (`natural: true`) so Play needs no species data. Everything else a feature says is shown (character page, Features tab) but not automated.
- Levelling: Play shows a link when the experience is there; the character page then offers the level. Hit points use the average of the Hit Die plus Constitution (a higher Constitution counts for earlier levels too); proficiency, power points, powers known and the highest power level come from the class table; an archetype is chosen at 3rd level (saved in `subclass` by id); Ability Score Improvement levels are the class's own (Fighter 4, 6, 8, 12, 14, 16, 19) and give two ability points or a feat. A character made at 3rd level or higher can take its archetype from the same panel.
- Creation: the background's language and tool choices and the species' extra language are picked in the Background step. Class and background starting equipment are not handed out because the app uses the Player's Handbook's starting wealth variant, which replaces them.
- Quests: `worldQuests` reads three off a new world (clear the most dangerous hostile site on the starting planet, recover a locked or hidden cache, reach the nearest other planet). Objectives name a target (`defeat`, `loot` or `reach` an area) and `advanceQuests` ticks them as the world changes; a finished quest pays credits sized against Wealth by Level. Objectives without a target (older campaigns) are ticked by hand.
- Ships: the party's ships are in the campaign's `starships` (`PartyShip`: a `Ship` with `fuel` and the berth `areaId`). House rules (not from the books; each is labelled in the game where it appears):
- Passage on someone else's ship: the fuel the trip burns at a Medium ship's fuel price (100 credits a unit); the shuttle between surface and orbit is free. The books have no fare table.
- A dead character can be revived with 1 hit point ("Revive (house rule)"), so a solo campaign can go on.
- A game master's reply cannot reduce hit points below 1 or kill; only a fight can.
- Enemies leave a downed character alone instead of attacking them, since the character is alone.
- One character flies any ship: crew sizes and deployments are not checked.
- Enemy ships can be taken once their guards are beaten.
- A look through a window from the street shows who is in that room, without a check.
- Contraband (`contrabandFrom`): spice and lightweapons are seized wherever there is law, explosives, poisons, slicing and forgery gear, restraining bolts and exotic weapons where it is policed, martial weapons and heavy armor where it is strict. Lawful traders do not sell what the law seizes and will not buy it; in lawless places merchants also sell spice and explosives. Buying contraband says so; nothing yet checks what the party carries.
- Haggling DC: 12, +2 for the black market, -1 per step of the trader's goodwill. Opening hours by building type, and the black market from 18:00 to 04:00.

- The watch (`ALERT_LEVELS`, `arrivalAlert`, `setAlert`, `watchDC` in shared/logic; `Area.alert`, `Area.allowed`, the building's `alarm`). Hostile creatures notice the party by degrees: unaware, suspicious, questioning, confronting, attacking. Coming in by a secret way: unaware. With "Move quietly" ticked: Dexterity (Stealth) against 10 + their best Wisdom modifier (+5 with the alarm up); made, unaware; missed by less than 5, suspicious; else as if walking in. Walking in: questioning at a building's entrance, confronting further in, attacking where the alarm is up; beasts and horrors do not talk and attack at once. Unaware or suspicious: moving on or taking a cache is a Stealth check and a miss moves the watch up a step; "Ambush" from unaware costs the enemy the first round and raises no alarm. Questioning: Bluff or Persuade (Charisma against 10 + their Wisdom + danger); success and they let the party pass (caches are then off limits), failure and they confront. Confronting: Intimidate at +5, go back the way you came, or fight. Attacking: the fight starts by itself. Any fight but an ambush raises the alarm through the building; a long rest clears alarms. Hostiles with nothing recorded (older saves, enemies the GM tab added) count as confronting, which is how the game behaved before.

Officers: 2 on the frontier, 3 to 4 policed, 5 to 7 strict, heavy troopers in strict law or for a heinous crime. The search covers the settlement (`districtOf`) for a time set by the crime (trespass 30 min, theft 60, brawl 120, assault 240, heinous 720) and the law (x0.5 frontier, x1 policed, x2 strict). Lawless space sends no one, unless the place is ruled by a crime syndicate: its enforcers come then. Raised by being seen picking a lock or forcing a door (trespass), taking a cache (theft), a blasted door (heinous), a brawl, and the lawmen turned on a party that was seen (see "The law in person"). Fighting hostile creatures is not a crime (it was until 2026-10-16: every fight sent officers, which made them come for a party defending itself). "Lie low" is a Stealth check against 12 + officers (+2 heavy); failing it is a fine of 100 x crime x law (x2 heavy). Leaving the district ends the search for the party.

Caught, every one of them turns hostile with a stat block (junior officer, trooper in strict law), the alarm goes up, the crime is reported and the fight begins by itself. The officers a search sends are real: at the scene a squad (the search's officers, as "Constables", "Security troopers" or "Syndicate enforcers") stands in the room hostile when the delay is up; entering another place of the district while the search is on is a Stealth check against the lie-low DC, one try a place, and failing it brings a squad. Only places of the law count: in a room where `danger` is above 0 (enemy ground) nobody minds a lock or a cache. At most 2 squads in a search (3 for heavy troopers or a cartel). Lying low still ends the search.
- Sight, leaving and the calm-down (2026-10-16; `SIGHT_SQUARES`, `leaveSight`, `calmedAt`, `inSight`, `blockedExit` in shared/logic; `Area.calmAt`; `leaveBy` in GameBoard). Supersedes the watch's "questioning: they bar the way" rule. Watchers see only 6 squares (about 30 feet; was 18, which put a whole district in sight, so a sentry on a far roof barred every exit). Nothing bars the way: leaving past watchers who can see the party is a Stealth check (`unseen`); a miss moves their watch up a step and the party may still go, unless that puts them on attack, and then the fight begins where they stand. When the party leaves a place the watchers there cannot see (`inSight`), that place's watch calms down after `WATCH_COOLDOWN` (10) minutes and goes back to unaware; coming back into their sight cancels it.
- The search on foot (2026-10-16; `Squad`, `startSquads`, `advanceSearch`, `searchNeighbours`, `squadSightDC` in shared/logic; the search effect in GameBoard). House rule. The squads a search sends (two, or three for heavy troopers or a cartel) start at the scene and walk the settlement's rooms and buildings (through gateways and the street between buildings), a room every `SEARCH_STEP` (2) minutes; a squad's first step is at the scene. A squad questions the people in each room it walks into, and where the party was seen before (`Pursuit.seen`) it learns who the party is (`identified`), then walks straight for the party's last known room (`known`). Where a squad walks in on the party (or on a roof or catwalk in its sight), the party rolls Stealth against `squadSightDC` (10, +2 for heavy troopers; 15 in a sheltered room); a miss means the squad closes in and the fight begins (`engageOfficers`, with the squad's count); a success lets it walk on.
- Criminal reputation (2026-10-16; `HEAT_POINTS`, `heatAt`, `heatLevel`, `addHeat`, `blockadeAt`; `World.heat`; the "Marked" line on the map). Two cooldowns run side by side. The short one is the search (`searchUntil`, as before). The long one is the settlement's heat: each crime adds points (trespass 1, theft and brawl 2, assault 3, heinous 5; only where there is law to report it) and a day takes one away. Level 1 (3 points) is known to the watch, level 2 (6) wanted, level 3 (9) marked: while a search is on at level 3 the officers hold the ways out of the settlement (`blockedExit`: leaving it is refused) and every vehicle or hired ride is blocked (on foot is still open), until the search is called off.
- Comms (2026-10-16; `hasCommlink`, `noteArrival`, `interceptCall`; `Pursuit.reports`; the call panel and the comm jammer in GameBoard). Only someone carrying a commlink calls over distance. A neutral civilian with a commlink who sees the party in a room that the search covers calls security, put through two minutes later (`REPORT_MINUTES`); the search then knows who the party is. A comm jammer switched on (an hour, from the card, as before) jams the district: no call goes through, the party's own calls to allies are blocked too, and no alarm goes up through the building. The party can stop a call on the card of the caller: "Talk them down" (Intimidate against 10 + the caller's Wisdom) or "Silence them", which turns the caller hostile and starts the fight. Friendly people do not call.
- Shelter (2026-10-16; `shelterOwner`, `shelterExits`, `grantShelter`). During a search, a room's owner (bartender, barkeep, innkeeper, resident, owner, landlord, proprietor or keeper, friendly) who is in the room can open a hidden or locked back room of the same building for the party: "Ask ... to open" is a Charisma (Persuade) check against 12 + the law. The way is unlocked and unhidden and the room becomes `sheltered`, where squads only find the party with a Stealth check against 15.
- The status line (the map, under the search): the number of squads on foot, whether they know who the party is, and what they last did (questioning people, a call, a walk-in). The squads are not drawn on the 3D map yet.
- Ways in and out kept open (2026-10-16, life 9; `streetGround`, `wayOutSquare`, `fixStreetWays`, `repairAccess` in shared/logic). Found by auditing the saved worlds: "Side entrance" doors were set on wall squares where furniture or a cache stood (16 of them in three worlds), and a building's way out could open on a pocket between buildings or under an upper floor that overhangs it (a hangar built to fit a ship stands over the concourse's door). Now a way out is only set on a wall square that nothing stands on, that opens on ground joined to the streets, and that leaves every way of the room reachable; the main ways the buildings were planned with are checked the same way and moved to another wall (or another ground-floor room) if they are dead. Worlds made before are mended once when Play opens them (`livenWorld`, `LIFE` 9: furniture, caches and ships moved off ways, dead ways moved or taken out). Checked on three saved worlds and six generated ones with an audit that walks every room's ways and every street door.
- Squads and barricades on the 3D map (2026-10-16; `Squads`, `Barricade` in LocalMap.tsx). The squads of the search for the party, and of the searches for locals (below), are drawn in the district: in front of the door of the building they are in (side by side where there are several), or up on the roof or catwalk they are on; they walk to their next place as the search moves them on (every two minutes of the clock) and go about a little on the spot. While a blockade is on (`blockadeAt`), each signpost for a way out of the district is walled in by a ring of striped barriers two squares out, with two officers behind them: the squares are not walkable, a tap on the sign or a barrier says why, and travellers going out or coming in wait at the barricade, "questioned at the blockade". Vehicles are barred by the exit list, as before.
- Traffic, lifts and incidents (2026-10-16, life 10; `outingVehicle`, `liftsBetween`, `rollIncident`, `advanceIncidents`, `closeIncident`, `incidentReward` in shared/logic). House rules. The day trips people make (`Routine.outing`) go by a vehicle (`outing.by`): speeder, airspeeder (long trips), mount (on worlds with beasts, short trips) or hauler (merchants); more people go (30%, merchants 50% and every 2 to 4 days) and the map draws what carries them. A party that wants to go where someone is going is offered a lift in the route list ("A lift with X, leaves at ..."): it leaves when they do, for a few credits. Once an hour of the clock, for the settlement the party is in, a crime may happen (about 1.3% an hour in a policed town, half that in lawless ones, never while another is open): a local steals, brawls, or takes a parked speeder. The culprit lies low in another room (a speeder thief flees to another settlement of the planet); constables search for them as for the party: squads walk the rooms, question whoever is in them, and a quarter of the time a witness tips them where to go; finding the culprit ends it, and so does the time running out. The culprit stands in their hiding room marked Wanted: the party can find them first and turn them in (the stolen sum, 25 x the law and three times that for a speeder, which goes back to its building). The kiosk's notices list what is open.
- Rides at houses and shops (2026-10-16; `placeVehicles`, `Vehicle.civil`). House rule. About half of the houses, shops, ports and workshops have a speeder parked outside, belonging to a local; a vehicle dealer has several. The party can hotwire one ("Steal it": Dexterity or Intelligence against 12 + the law, a security or slicer's kit if trained): seen by a lawman, or failing, it is theft and the law is called; done quietly, nobody knows. Locals can take one too (above).
- Droid dealers (2026-10-16; `droidStock`, `droidPrice`, `droidOffer`, `droidRepairPrice`, `droidShop` in GameBoard). House rule, the books give no droid prices. The showroom and repair bay of a droid dealer sell the droid stat blocks of the rules data (44 of them) at 6 credits an experience point (150 at least), four to six on the floor a day; droids built for war (security, battle, assassin, tactical, probe, ...) only where the law is frontier or lawless, or from the black market at 1.6 times; nothing dearer than the settlement carries. A bought droid follows the party as a companion. The dealer buys the party's droids back at 40% of the price (less for the hurt), mends one for 5 credits a hit point plus 5 per point of challenge (half an hour), and fits the droid customizations the party carries for the surgeon's fee (100 credits an hour, 8 hours by size), with no check and no kit. The motor upgrade is as before. Customizations themselves are still not sold over a counter (Wretched Hives: a downtime activity).
- Ship crew (2026-10-16; `seatsOf`, `crewOf`, `PartyShip.crew`; the "Stations" list in the Aboard panel). House rule. The seats of a ship are the stations on its deck (helm, co-pilot, navigation, engineering, and one to three gunnery seats by size). Each seat has a menu of the party; a member sits in one seat. In a ship fight the character flies, and each other living party member (not a beast) seated at a station gives the ship one more action (up to four), and each one at a gunnery seat is a gunner, whose shots are not made from the helm; when a companion has the helm, the character at a station counts. A ship whose crew was never assigned is crewed by the first companions in order, gunnery seats first, as before.
- Cameras, panels and kiosks (2026-10-16, life 10; `useKindOf`, `camerasIn`, `addPanels`; the asset card in GameBoard). House rule. Rooms in the plans now get a security camera, an alarm panel, an access panel or an info kiosk by what the room is (offices, halls, command rooms, vaults and cells); older worlds are given them once. A camera records a crime done in front of it in a place with law 2 or more (the law is called, a moment after the act) unless the cameras are looped: "Loop it for an hour" is Intelligence or Dexterity against 12 + the law (a security or slicer's kit if trained). The alarm panel silences or trips the building's alarm; the access panel opens every locked door of the building (Intelligence against 12 + the law; a miss reports trespass); the kiosk lists the wanted, what the search knows, and the settlement's heat. Info kiosks and camera poles also stand in the streets of urban settlements. Rooms are about 28% larger by area (each side one square more, the maximum two), because the districts had a good deal of empty ground; older worlds keep their sizes.
- Passage off world (`passageOffers`, `passageCheckDC`, `stowawayFine`, `SMUGGLER_SHARE`; `passagePanel`, `bookPassage` in GameBoard; life 7 of `livenWorld` adds the people). House rule, the books price no fares. A ticket agent (the "Arrivals concourse", "Port office" or "Airlock" of a port; was the dockmaster) sells a berth to any planet the lanes reach: the fuel the trip burns at a Medium ship's price, plus half an hour to board (an hour more by shuttle from a port that is not the planet's gateway); the party arrives in orbit. A smuggler (each port settlement has one, in the bar) sells it at 60% with a Deception check against 10 + 2 x law to fool the customs scan (none in lawless space); failing it reports trespass and nothing is paid. "Stow away" is free: Stealth against 12 + 2 x law, failing it is a fine (50 x law) and the same report. The exit buttons' passage from orbit stays as it was.
- Heist countdown is 45 minutes (was 25: the stages' own minutes ate it), and opening the lockout in time counts even if the clock ran out during that stage. Heist DCs came down (the old ones were close to impossible at low level): 10 + law for customs, terminal and guards, 12 + law for the lockout (were 12 + 2 x law, 14 + 2 x law).
- Hired rides (`groundRides`, `hasRidesForHire`): any settlement with a type that has traffic gives one, from the settlement or any building in it: 2 credits + 1 per 10 km in a city, town or station (was 5 + 1 per 4 km, and only standing in the street); a village or outpost has a hauler at double the fare and a half hour more to find (was none).
- Taking a ship (`startHeist`, `heistStep`, `takeShip` in shared/logic; `World.heist`). House rule, the books give none. A ship that is not an enemy's can be bought, or stolen through four stages: customs (bribe at 2% of the price times law + 1, or forge a clearance: Deception), the hangar terminal (slice it: Intelligence; this starts a countdown of 25 minutes to take-off), the guards (intimidate or charm: Charisma; or fight: Strength or Dexterity, a lost fight is capture), the lockout (overcome it: Intelligence with a slicer's kit). A failed check raises the alarm and can be retried; four failures in all catch the party. The countdown runs on the clock whatever is done, and the ship lifts off without them when it runs out. Taking the ship is a heinous crime.

- Districts (2026-10-15, life 6; `districtsOf`, `planDistrict`, `DistrictPlan`, `World.districtPlans` in shared/logic; `BuildingScene` in LocalMap.tsx). The user's design: a district is four to six FULL buildings with several ways in, catwalks between them and exterior ladders to the roofs, on one shared 3D map that the party and the people walk across, with the street and the roofs in sight of each other. `districtsOf` puts a settlement's buildings in a line by theme (civic: palace, garrison, archive, command post; market: market, workshop, warehouse, residences, tenements, dealers, medcenter; docks: spaceport, gates, cantina, hideout, transit hub, shipyard) and by place, and cuts it into as few districts as hold it (four to six where the settlement has the buildings; wild sites have none). New optional buildings fill them: tenements, transit hub, droid dealer, vehicle dealer, shipyard. `planDistrict` moves every building's rooms onto one grid (alleys of 3 squares, avenues of 6, a street round the whole), gives each building of three rooms or more a second way in ("Side entrance", a gateway room), a roof (a room of form `roof`, open air, furnished with cover and given sentries: hostile ones where the building is hostile; reached by a ladder up the outside wall, `Roof` is a gateway room of the building so "Climb the ladder to Roof" is an exit of the street), and catwalks (rooms of form `walkway`, joining two roofs at the same height up to 12 squares apart; the validator allows a "Catwalk" path between buildings). Worlds before life 6 get all of it when Play opens them (`livenWorld`). The scene draws every building of the district, the lots they stand on, the roofs with their cover and sentries, the ladders (tap the pad at the foot) and the catwalks on posts; the party walks the street freely and goes in by any way in of any building (`go` carries the squares walked, a minute for fifty); `usePartySpot` is on the district's grid.
- Fights in the open (`districtArena`, `levelsArena`, `watchersAt`, `exposedRooms`; `Arena.platforms`, `links`, `district` and `Combatant.z` in useCombat.ts). House rules; the books give no elevation. Height is in levels of 10 feet; two squares make a level, and the greatest of horizontal and vertical squares is the distance. A ladder or stair costs 10 feet a level, either way. Buildings are blocks with a height that a shot cannot pass; whoever stands on a roof shoots over its edge squares (the squares further in stop a shot to or from the street). Cover is what stands at the target's own height within 5 feet of it. In the street, on a roof or on a catwalk every hostile on the district's roofs and catwalks is a watcher (`watchersAt`: arrival alert, the watch's DC, who bars the way, who joins a fight), and a fight there is fought on the district's own map by the same scene (`DuelPieces`: lit squares at their height, fighters on theirs). A room with a gallery fights as one place (the gallery a raised floor of half a level, the stairs the way up); a hostile building keeps a sentry on its gallery.
- Pits (`furnishLevels`, `trapsIn`, `Path.climb`, `Exit.climb`): some rooms (3 in 10 in hostile or wild places, 1 in 12 elsewhere) have a loose flagstone over a pit (a room of form `pit` below, level -1). Passive Perception notices it from two squares (DC 12 + the room's danger) and Search finds it; stepping on it drops the character a level (1d6, never the last hit point outside a fight) into the pit, a minute spent. The pit has a way out up to the room: Athletics or Dexterity against DC 12, tried again as often as it takes, a minute for each try.
- The master (`pathToTeach`, `teachingFor`, `rollEncounter` with kind `master`). Once the party has renown of 3 or more with the settlement's faction, a master may be waiting in a district (once for each settlement) who teaches the path the character lacks: the Force to one who has tech powers, tech to one who has the Force, and to one who has neither whichever suits their Force alignment and skills (`leaningOf`). The lessons are an at-will power and a power of the first level, of the character's alignment where the path has one (the master's two days of teaching and 200 credits a level), and a pool of power points to cast them from: the character's level plus the casting ability's modifier, at least two, from the same pool a caster's are. Renown is earned by helping people, jobs for fixers and the quests of the settlement's faction. The other encounters of a district are someone in trouble (Wisdom, Medicine, DC 12), a gang that means to rob the party (talk or fight, a brawl reported to the law) and a fixer with a job (a package to take to another building of the district).

### Gathering and materials (2026-10-16, all house rules)
SW5e has no harvesting or mining rules; it has the Scavenging, Bioanalysis and Archaeologist kits, Jeweler's and Tinker's implements, and the Crafting downtime (raw materials worth half the item's value). The game fills the gap (`shared/logic`, "Gathering and materials"):
- Caves, ruins and wrecks hold **resource nodes** (ore, plants, crystals, salvage) as `LootCache.node`, placed by the room codes `N:ore|plant|crystal|salvage` and drawn on the 3D map as a vein, patch, cluster or heap. Worlds made before them get them from `livenWorld` (life 11). Tier 0-3 = Standard to Advanced; DC 10/14/18/22 (the book's blueprint DCs).
- A harvest is a check (10 minutes) with the family's kit (`FAMILIES`); without a kit only tier 0, at +5. Each 5 over the DC and a trained kit add a unit. A node has 2-4 harvests, a natural 1 spoils one, it recovers 3 days after it ran dry.
- Materials are stacks in `playState.materials`. The Crafting activity pays raw materials with them at their value (the dearest unit that fits first) and the rest in credits. **Refine** turns 4 units into one refined unit worth 1.5x the four, with a kit and a check at the tier's DC.
- **Salvage (2026-10-16):** beasts and horrors that fall leave a carcass (a fifth family, hides and parts: `makeRemains`, a node that is worked once and does not come back) in the room, placed on the floor by `withCacheAsset`; a powered-down droid has a "Strip for parts" button (salvage family, the droid goes); a destroyed enemy ship also gives parts worth a twentieth of its value (salvage and ore) beside the credits. The tier follows the Challenge Rating like the book's rare ingredients (`tierOfCr`).
- **What a thing can be made of:** each tool works its own families (`familiesForTool`: Armstech's ore and parts, Biochemist's plants and hides, Jeweler's crystal and ore, Tinker's anything); an enhanced item by its type (`familiesForEnhanced`). Only those families count toward the raw materials; the rest is paid in credits.
- **Who rolls and with which skill (2026-10-16):** every ability check and tool check (`abilityCheck`, `toolRoll`, and Crafting's) opens `components/Play/CheckDialog.tsx`: the player picks the party member (the character, or a companion with a stat block: its bonus is read from the stat block's skills and modifiers) and one of the skills that bear on the check (by ability, or the tool's own), with the bonus shown for each; a trained tool adds its proficiency (no stacking with the skill's) and, with a trained skill, gives advantage (the existing house rule). "Not now" calls the check off without consequence. A box in the dialog turns it off for good (the character then rolls with the best skill; local storage key `sw5e.autoChecks`); the play-through test sets it. Downtime activities other than Crafting still roll with the character's own `checkFor`.
- Not done: species/background gathering perks (Defel, Scavenger...), hazards, gathering in settlement districts, and a browser (e2e) step for harvesting and for the dialog.

## SW5e.com crosscheck (2026-10-12; the content and formula mismatches listed here were fixed on 2026-10-13 by the import, except where "Gaps that remain" says otherwise)
How it was done: sw5e.com is a single-page app over a public API, `https://sw5eapi.azurewebsites.net/api/<name>` (data version 199, unchanged since 2023-05-06). Every dataset was downloaded and compared by script with a JSON dump of `client/src/lib/sw5e/*.ts`. Dataset names: species, class, archetype, background, feat, power, equipment, enhancedItem, monster, maneuvers, feature, fightingStyle, fightingMastery, lightsaberForm, classImprovement, multiclassImprovement, splashclassImprovement, weaponFocus, weaponSupremacy, weaponProperty, armorProperty, skills, conditions, characterAdvancement, referenceTable, starshipBaseSize, starshipDeployment, starshipEquipment, starshipModification, starshipVenture, PlayerHandbookRule, StarshipRule, WretchedHivesRule, VariantRule, ExpandedContent, dataVersion.

Result in one line: the formulas copied from D&D 5e are right; almost all bundled content is invented or mis-statted, and the SW5e-specific rules (power points, powers known, background feat, alignment) are wrong.

Matches the site: ability modifier, proficiency bonus by level, XP table, point buy (27 points, same costs), standard array, HP (full hit die at level 1, average after), hit dice of all seven classes, unarmored AC 10 + Dex, Strength for melee and Dexterity for ranged, critical hits double the dice, carrying capacity Strength x 15, archetype at level 3, 15 of 16 condition names (Shocked has the wrong effects; conditions.ts is not loaded by the app).

Wrong in code the app uses:
- Saving throws: Guardian is Constitution/Charisma (code: Strength/Constitution); Scout is Strength/Dexterity (code: Dexterity/Wisdom).
- Skills (constants.ts SKILLS): "Endurance" does not exist, "Animal Handling" is missing, Nature and Piloting are Intelligence skills (code: Wisdom, Dexterity). Class lists name History, Science, Religion, which are not SW5e skills. Consular picks 2 skills (code 3). No class skill list matches the site exactly.
- Class proficiencies and features: mostly D&D class features under SW5e class names (Ki, Natural Explorer, Favored Enemy, Aura of Protection). Armor and weapon proficiencies differ for every class.
- Power points (deriveStats in pages/CharacterCreation.tsx, calculateMaxPowerPoints in rules.ts): site is Consular level x 4, Sentinel x 3, Guardian x 2, plus Wisdom or Charisma modifier; Engineer level x 2 and Scout level x 1 (from level 2) plus Intelligence modifier. Code gives level + modifier and nothing to Scout.
- Powers known at level 1 (PowersSelection.tsx): site Consular 9, Sentinel 7, Guardian 5, Engineer 6, Scout 0. Code: Consular 2, Sentinel 1 force + 1 tech, Guardian 1, Engineer 2. It gives tech powers to Scholar and Sentinel, which have none on the site.
- Max power level: site full casters reach 9th (1st, 1st, 2nd, 2nd, 3rd ...). Code caps at 5 with ceil(level / 4). calculatePowerSlots is D&D spell slots; SW5e has no slots.
- Species (10 of the site's 141; 5 of the 30 in the Player's Handbook): ability bonuses are wrong for Advozse, Arcona, Human, Twi'lek, Zabrak and Bothan, so deriveStats saves wrong scores for them. Human is +2 to one and +1 to two of the player's choice (or +1 to four), not +1 Strength and +1 Dexterity. Aleena speed is 25 (code 35). 20 of the 23 trait names are invented.
- Backgrounds: every background grants a feat at level 1 and a choice of 2 skills from 4. The code grants no feat (FeatsSelection allows floor(level / 4), so 0 at level 1) and never applies background skills. Ace Pilot, Diplomat, Outsider and the picker's Scholar, Pilot, Merchant are not on the site.
- Feats: 4 of the 13 in feats.ts exist (Alert, Force-Sensitive, Linguist, Tough) and 1 of the 5 the picker shows.
- Powers: 9 of 13 force powers exist by name and only 2 have the right level (Force Storm is 9th level, code 5th). None of the 11 tech powers exists.
- Equipment (equipment.ts, items.ts): 14 of 26 and 13 of 27 names exist; one price in each file matches. Blaster pistol is 175 cr (code 450 and 500), lightsaber 450 cr and 1d6 (code 3000 and 2d8), vibroblade 1d8 (code 1d4). Starting credits are a flat 500; the site uses class starting equipment or a roll such as 8d4 x 100 cr for a Fighter.
- Alignment: site is Light, Dark or Balanced combined with Lawful, Chaotic or Neutral. The code's five options are not SW5e.
- Combat in Play: a natural 1 does not auto-miss; the attack bonus is modifier + 2 at every level; damage is d8 melee and d6 ranged whatever weapon is carried; 0 HP is "defeated" with no death saves; Rest is a free long rest. Test Combat's Stormtrooper (30 HP, AC 13, +3) and Imperial Officer (20 HP, AC 12, +2) are not the site's Trooper (13 HP, AC 15, +5, 1d8+3) and Junior Officer (13 HP, AC 12, +3, 1d6+1), each CR 1/2.

Present on the site, absent or token in code: 137 archetypes (code 13, three real names, invented features), 119 maneuvers (2, neither real), 32 fighting styles and 32 masteries (1 real name), 20 lightsaber forms (0), 46 weapon and 30 armor properties (2), 1918 enhanced items (0), 271 monsters (15 entries, none with the site's numbers), Exhaustion condition, multiclassing. Starships: the site builds a ship from size (Tiny to Gargantuan), tier, hull and shield dice, six crew deployments, 257 modifications, 104 equipment items and 67 ventures; the code has five fixed stat blocks with sizes like "Colossal" that the site does not use.

Site pages with no counterpart: Tools is only a login-gated Character Creator (steps Species, Class, Ability Scores, Description, Equipment; Standard Array, Point Buy or Manual, same three methods as here). Assets is PDFs of the four rulebooks, character, deployment and starship sheets, expanded-content links, a podcast.

## Decisions
- Package is ESM.
- Storage: Postgres on this VM with node-postgres (user accepted the default).
- Deleted CharacterCreationForm.tsx (dead, corrupted, unreferenced); pages/CharacterCreation.tsx is the creation flow.
- No 2D maps (user, 2026-10-13): what is where is shown on the 3D map and used by tapping it. Lists and buttons in the HUD stay, as another way to do the same things.
- The server binds loopback by default (2c). Phase 3 decides what sits in front of it.
- The class picker offers only classes that have data (2c). Revisit when data for the other three is added.
- Playwright is a dev dependency, pinned (2c).
- Rules data is generated, never hand-written (2d). All ten classes are offered again now that all have data.
- New campaigns always get a generated world; campaigns without one are offered one in Play (changed 2026-10-13; they kept a plain location list before).
- The generator's code lives in the existing shared/logic module and the editor in the existing CampaignManager, at the user's request to extend files rather than add them. The only new files are the import script and the generated data.
- Access is Tailscale-only (user, 2026-10-13); public HTTPS stays a possible later development. No Oracle port is opened for the game.
- Phase 2f added no source files: `lib/sw5e/characterCalculation.ts` (an unused helper with two hard-coded experience tables) was rewritten as the module for features and levelling. New files are `deploy/*` and migration 0001.
- Rules the books leave open are settled by the labelled house rules under "Play rules". Starting equipment follows the starting wealth variant.
