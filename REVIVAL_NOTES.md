## Rules data: one source (shared/data)
- `npm run import:sw5e` (shared/scripts/import-sw5e.ts) downloads every dataset from `https://sw5eapi.azurewebsites.net/api` and writes `shared/data/sw5e/<name>.json` plus `manifest.json` (data version 199, 36 datasets, 9.2 MB, about 15 seconds). It strips storage metadata and duplicate "...Json" / "...Enum" fields, repairs the API's broken dashes and apostrophes, gives every record an `id`, and stores class, archetype and species features with their owner. `SW5E_CACHE=<dir>` reads saved responses instead of the network.
- It also derives two small files from book text: `travel.json` (the nine galactic regions, the hyperspace travel-time table, realspace times, starship size table) and `lookups.json` (languages, the nine alignments, point-buy costs, wealth by level).
- Two more files are derived from book text (2026-10-15): `transport.json` (the Expanded Content's mounts, tack and vehicles tables and the Player's Handbook travel pace table: speeds, costs, carrying capacity, miles an hour) and `tools.json` (Wretched Hives' Tool Proficiencies chapter: for each tool the skills it helps, its special use and its sample activities with their DCs). `DERIVE_ONLY=1 npm run import:sw5e` rebuilds just those two from the files on disk, without the network.
- Do not edit `shared/data/sw5e/*.json` and do not define rules content anywhere else. Re-run the importer to refresh.
- `shared/data/index.ts`: `loadDataset(name)`, one lazy loader per dataset. `client/src/lib/sw5e/{species,classes,archetypes,backgrounds,feats,forcePowers,techPowers,equipment,items,monsters,npcs,droids,vehicles,starships,maneuvers,fightingStyles,weaponProperties,conditions,skills,exploits,constants}.ts` keep their old export names but only map that data. `client/src/lib/data/*.ts` re-export them.
- Server: `server/sw5eData.ts` loads datasets on demand; `GET /api/sw5e` (manifest) and `GET /api/sw5e/:dataset?search=&fields=&limit=`.
- Bundle: pages load lazily (App.tsx), so the home page script fell from 1.9 MB to 0.4 MB; character creation loads a 2.6 MB chunk (0.5 MB gzipped) with the data it needs.
- The setting's known systems and planets (`client/src/lib/sw5e/starSystems.ts`, `locations.ts`) are not rules data and are not from sw5e.com; the world generator uses them as seeds.

## World generator (shared/logic/index.ts)
- Model: `World { areas, paths, startId, antagonist, partyLevel, ... }`. An `Area` has a kind (galaxy, region, system, planet, settlement, building, room), a parent, a position, a danger level, `gateway` (the way in and out of its parent) and its contents: `occupants`, `ships`, `loot`. A `Path` joins two areas of the same kind: hyperlane, sublight, route, street or door, with a travel time or distance and optional `locked` DC and `hidden`.
- Every area is a place to stand: a room, the street in front of a building, the outskirts of a settlement, a planet's orbit, a system's jump point. `exitsOf` lists the moves from an area (along a path, in through a gateway, out to the parent); `findPath` gives the route between any two areas. Regions only group systems.
- How it builds: regions and hyperspace times from `travel.json`; known systems placed in their regions and generated ones added so no region is empty; hyperlanes as a minimum spanning tree per region plus one lane between neighbouring regions and a few shortcuts; planets on sublight lanes; then for a planet: a starport settlement, a second settlement and one or two wilderness sites suited to the biome, joined by routes; buildings by settlement type joined by streets; rooms by building type with fixed layouts, locked and hidden doors. Only the starting planet is built in full; `expandArea` builds the rest when someone goes there.
- Placement, all from the rules data: staff by what a room is for, with gear picked by name from the equipment data; faction sentries and hostile garrisons from the stat blocks a faction fields; beasts whose text matches the planet's biome; horrors in ruins; utility droids in ports and workshops; fights sized by the 5th edition XP budgets for the party's level and size and the area's danger; stat-block loadouts read from their attacks and armor; ships assembled from starship equipment by size; loot caches by room purpose, with credits sized against the Wealth by Level table and enhanced items of a rarity suited to the level.
- `validateWorld(world, catalog?)` lists what is wrong: wrong nesting, paths between different kinds or parents, a container without a gateway, areas unreachable from the start, and (with the catalog) any stat block or item that is not in the rules data.
- Edit functions keep the map sound and say why when they refuse: `updateArea`, `addArea` (joined to its nearest neighbour), `removeArea` (neighbours are re-joined), `connectAreas`, `disconnectAreas` (refused if it would cut the map), `setGateway`, `setStart`.
- Server (`server/sw5eRoutes.ts`): `POST /api/world/generate | expand | populate | validate | occupant`. The world is saved in the campaign's `galaxyMap` column; `currentLocation` is then an area id.
- UI: the campaign page (`components/Campaign/CampaignManager.tsx`) generates, browses and edits the world and now saves everything it edits. Play (`pages/GameBoard.tsx`) shows the current area, who and what is there, and its exits; enemies bar the way onward until beaten; fights use the stat block's AC, HP, attack and damage and the weapons the character carries; the fallen leave their gear; loot goes to the character; locks and hidden things take ability checks; `Explore` builds an unvisited place. The Play galaxy map draws the world's systems and hyperlanes.
- The same seed and options give the same world.

- Floors (life 4; "Levels" in shared/logic: `levelsOf`, `linkLevels`, `planBuilding`). Rooms have `layout.level`: 0 the ground, below 0 beneath it (cellar, basement, crypt, vault, detention block, hidden compartment, escape tunnel), above 0 over it (apartments, quarters, offices, command and operations rooms, control tower, rooftop, lookout, bridge, port office), and a gallery half a floor over a tall room (main bar, workfloor, storage floor, reading hall, ...). Anything that holds a ship (or is called a docking bay, hangar, berth, arm) is on the building's top floor (at least the first above ground; landing fields and pads are the ground) and has a `roof`: `open` to the sky, or `hangar` (doors that slide back, animated) where the air is hostile (volcanic, ice, barren, gas giant, ocean, stations). Rooms on different floors are not joined directly: a room of its own lies between them (`layout.form`: since 2026-10-20 a stair block in a built building, see the next point; a lift where a ship room is one end; a rising passage in the wild; `climb` says which way the floor rises), plus now and then corridors (large buildings), elevated walkways (upper floors of urban, ocean and station buildings), a ladder up the outside wall (a gateway room, a second way in to an upper room) and a gallery with a flight of `stairs` assets either side. Nothing is ever put in those (furnishing skips them; a gallery is furnished). Rooms on other floors may stand over each other (`clash` compares floors as well as squares). The 3D map draws each room at its floor, the party climbs the steps, what is over a room being looked into is taken away and the ground is taken away from a cellar. Worlds before life 4 get all of it once: every built building is planned again (the secret ways the party found stay found).
- Stair blocks (2026-10-20, the user's design; "Stair blocks" and `linkColumns` in shared/logic, `tests/world/access.test.ts`). Rooms over and under one another stand on one footprint, a column: a ground room (the host), the rooms stacked over it (`stack:<id>`, a tower has several) and a basement under it (`under:<id>`). `linkColumns` puts every room over the ground on a column top (the floor it names, else the nearest: a rooftop over the apartments) and every room under it under a ground room without one (the room it opens off first), never a way in from the street (`levelsOf` keeps a gateway on the ground), a tall room, a hangar or a round room; a column with nothing over or under its host gets an "Upper storeroom" or a "Storage cellar", so that every block is five lanes. Passages of a column's rooms to rooms outside it move down to their hosts (the block is the way between its floors); the lock or secret of a room's way in stays on the door into it at its stairs (`entry:` tags). The block (`stairBlock`) stands against a side of the host, five squares wide and `BLOCK_LENGTH` (4) long away from it, in lanes: upReturn, upStart, hall, downStart, downReturn (the other way about in some buildings). The hall is a corridor at the ground floor: the host's door at its near end, at its far end the next room the host opened onto (that passage moves to the hall) or a dead end. The two flights up are one room, a switchback (`layout.switchback`, form stairs, climbing one level): up the low lane from its foot (a doorway in the host's wall) to the landing (the far row, half a floor up), and back up the other lane to its head, a doorway into the room over the host; the two flights down are the same from the basement's doorway up to their head, a doorway in the host's wall. So the host's wall has three doorways side by side: the flight up, the hall, the flight down (at first the flights opened off the hall's first square, beside the host's door, but three doors on one square could not be told apart by a tap). A tower has a switchback over the first for each floor more, its foot a door from the room on the floor below. Every flight has a doorway at its foot and at its head (the landing could take a door to a half-level room; none is made yet). `planBuilding` sets a column on its host's footprint and its block against the host's wall as soon as the host has its place (`settle`, `placeBlock`, before anything else is put round the host; the host is at least 5 by 5); if no side fits, a lift beside the host instead (`liftInstead`; none in the test worlds). The mezzanine keeps its straight in-room flight (`toward` on the stairs asset), drawn with a doorway at its foot and at its top. Taking stairs: a tap walks to the foot of a flight nobody stands on (`stairsApproach`; it used to walk onto the stairs' own blocked squares, so the mezzanine could not be reached, fixed `f762739`), and arriving the party stands at the way back (`arrivalSquares`). Since the evening of 2026-10-20 the block stacks both ways (`stairBlock(host, side, at, mirror, ups, downs)`): a switchback up for each floor over the host (city towers of three to five floors, `MAX_HEIGHT` 5) and one down for each floor under it (sub-basements, `MAX_DEPTH` 2; vaults, crypts and cells go under a basement where there is one); doors keyed `foot-up<n>`/`head-up<n>`/`head-down<n>`/`foot-down<n>`; the block's side, offset and mirroring are kept on its hall (`geo:`). A roof on a column's top floor (planDistrict prefers one) gets a switchback more, from the top floor to the roof, with the roof door at its head (`roofStairs`): a way in from the roof's ladder. Windows have a `pane` (size, latch, lock DC, sensor) and lead to their building (`to`), so arriving through one stands the party at it; balconies (form `balcony`, open air, not a way in) hang off the great houses' upper rooms over the street, joined to their room by patio doors (a path that may be `alarmed`) and reached only that way: no climb from the street (grapple hooks will, once written). The camera follows the party up from the street at once (the street's follow point was cleared a frame late, which left the camera aimed at the street after a roof's ladder). The audit (`auditBuildings`) checks columns (footprint, a floor apart, a door to the block), blocks (against the host, five lanes, switchbacks of two lanes) and that the floors either side of every door are within a step (a sixth of a floor), and walks every building (`walkBuilding`): from every way in, over the squares the map lets a player walk, through every door and up and down every flight; every room reached and every way usable. - The watch (`ALERT_LEVELS`, `arrivalAlert`, `setAlert`, `watchDC` in shared/logic; `Area.alert`, `Area.allowed`, the building's `alarm`). Hostile creatures notice the party by degrees: unaware, suspicious, questioning, confronting, attacking. Coming in by a secret way: unaware. With "Move quietly" ticked: Dexterity (Stealth) against 10 + their best Wisdom modifier (+5 with the alarm up); made, unaware; missed by less than 5, suspicious; else as if walking in. Walking in: questioning at a building's entrance, confronting further in, attacking where the alarm is up; beasts and horrors do not talk and attack at once. Unaware or suspicious: moving on or taking a cache is a Stealth check and a miss moves the watch up a step; "Ambush" from unaware costs the enemy the first round and raises no alarm. Questioning: Bluff or Persuade (Charisma against 10 + their Wisdom + danger); success and they let the party pass (caches are then off limits), failure and they confront. Confronting: Intimidate at +5, go back the way you came, or fight. Attacking: the fight starts by itself. Any fight but an ambush raises the alarm through the building; a long rest clears alarms. Hostiles with nothing recorded (older saves, enemies the GM tab added) count as confronting, which is how the game behaved before.

Raised by being seen picking a lock or forcing a door (trespass), taking a cache (theft), a blasted door (heinous), a brawl, and the lawmen turned on a party that was seen (see "The law in person"). Fighting hostile creatures is not a crime (it was until 2026-10-16: every fight sent officers, which made them come for a party defending itself). "Lie low" is a Stealth check against 12 + officers (+2 heavy); failing it is a fine of 100 x crime x law (x2 heavy). Leaving the district ends the search for the party.

### Gathering and materials (2026-10-16, all house rules)
SW5e has no harvesting or mining rules; it has the Scavenging, Bioanalysis and Archaeologist kits, Jeweler's and Tinker's implements, and the Crafting downtime (raw materials worth half the item's value). The game fills the gap (`shared/logic`, "Gathering and materials"):
- Caves, ruins and wrecks hold **resource nodes** (ore, plants, crystals, salvage) as `LootCache.node`, placed by the room codes `N:ore|plant|crystal|salvage` and drawn on the 3D map as a vein, patch, cluster or heap. Worlds made before them get them from `livenWorld` (life 11). Tier 0-3 = Standard to Advanced; DC 10/14/18/22 (the book's blueprint DCs).
- A harvest is a check (10 minutes) with the family's kit (`FAMILIES`); without a kit only tier 0, at +5. Each 5 over the DC and a trained kit add a unit. A node has 2-4 harvests, a natural 1 spoils one, it recovers 3 days after it ran dry.
- Materials are stacks in `playState.materials`. The Crafting activity pays raw materials with them at their value (the dearest unit that fits first) and the rest in credits. **Refine** turns 4 units into one refined unit worth 1.5x the four, with a kit and a check at the tier's DC.
- **Salvage (2026-10-16):** beasts and horrors that fall leave a carcass (a fifth family, hides and parts: `makeRemains`, a node that is worked once and does not come back) in the room, placed on the floor by `withCacheAsset`; a powered-down droid has a "Strip for parts" button (salvage family, the droid goes); a destroyed enemy ship also gives parts worth a twentieth of its value (salvage and ore) beside the credits. The tier follows the Challenge Rating like the book's rare ingredients (`tierOfCr`).
- **What a thing can be made of:** each tool works its own families (`familiesForTool`: Armstech's ore and parts, Biochemist's plants and hides, Jeweler's crystal and ore, Tinker's anything); an enhanced item by its type (`familiesForEnhanced`). Only those families count toward the raw materials; the rest is paid in credits.
- **Who rolls and with which skill (2026-10-16):** every ability check and tool check (`abilityCheck`, `toolRoll`, and Crafting's) opens `components/Play/CheckDialog.tsx`: the player picks the party member (the character, or a companion with a stat block: its bonus is read from the stat block's skills and modifiers) and one of the skills that bear on the check (by ability, or the tool's own), with the bonus shown for each; a trained tool adds its proficiency (no stacking with the skill's) and, with a trained skill, gives advantage (the existing house rule). "Not now" calls the check off without consequence. A box in the dialog turns it off for good (the character then rolls with the best skill; local storage key `sw5e.autoChecks`); the play-through test sets it. Downtime activities other than Crafting still roll with the character's own `checkFor`.
- Not done: species/background gathering perks (Defel, Scavenger...), hazards, gathering in settlement districts, and a browser (e2e) step for harvesting and for the dialog.

How it was done: sw5e.com is a single-page app over a public API, `https://sw5eapi.azurewebsites.net/api/<name>` (data version 199, unchanged since 2023-05-06). Every dataset was downloaded and compared by script with a JSON dump of `client/src/lib/sw5e/*.ts`. Dataset names: species, class, archetype, background, feat, power, equipment, enhancedItem, monster, maneuvers, feature, fightingStyle, fightingMastery, lightsaberForm, classImprovement, multiclassImprovement, splashclassImprovement, weaponFocus, weaponSupremacy, weaponProperty, armorProperty, skills, conditions, characterAdvancement, referenceTable, starshipBaseSize, starshipDeployment, starshipEquipment, starshipModification, starshipVenture, PlayerHandbookRule, StarshipRule, WretchedHivesRule, VariantRule, ExpandedContent, dataVersion.

Result in one line: the formulas copied from D&D 5e are right; almost all bundled content is invented or mis-statted, and the SW5e-specific rules (power points, powers known, background feat, alignment) are wrong.

Matches the site: ability modifier, proficiency bonus by level, XP table, point buy (27 points, same costs), standard array, HP (full hit die at level 1, average after), hit dice of all seven classes, unarmored AC 10 + Dex, Strength for melee and Dexterity for ranged, critical hits double the dice, carrying capacity Strength x 15, archetype at level 3, 15 of 16 condition names (Shocked has the wrong effects; conditions.ts is not loaded by the app).

Wrong in code the app uses:
- Saving throws: Guardian is Constitution/Charisma (code: Strength/Constitution); Scout is Strength/Dexterity (code: Dexterity/Wisdom).
- Skills (constants.ts SKILLS): "Endurance" does not exist, "Animal Handling" is missing, Nature and Piloting are Intelligence skills (code: Wisdom, Dexterity). Class lists name History, Science, Religion, which are not SW5e skills. Consular picks 2 skills (code 3). No class skill list matches the site exactly.
- Class proficiencies and features: mostly D&D class features under SW5e class names (Ki, Natural Explorer, Favored Enemy, Aura of Protection). Armor and weapon proficiencies differ for every class.
- Power points (deriveStats in pages/CharacterCreation.tsx, calculateMaxPowerPoints in rules.ts): site is Consular level x 4, Sentinel x 3, Guardian x 2, plus Wisdom or Charisma modifier; Engineer level x 2 and Scout level x 1 (from level 2) plus Intelligence modifier. Code gives level + modifier and nothing to Scout.
- Powers known at level 1 (PowersSelection.tsx): site Consular 9, Sentinel 7, Guardian 5, Engineer 6, Scout 0. Code: Consular 2, Sentinel 1 force + 1 tech, Guardian 1, Engineer 2. It gives tech powers to Scholar and Sentinel, which have none on the site.
- Max power level: site full casters reach 9th (1st, 1st, 2nd, 2nd, 3rd ...). Code caps at 5 with ceil(level / 4). calculatePowerSlots is D&D spell slots; SW5e has no slots.
- Species (10 of the site's 141; 5 of the 30 in the Player's Handbook): ability bonuses are wrong for Advozse, Arcona, Human, Twi'lek, Zabrak and Bothan, so deriveStats saves wrong scores for them. Human is +2 to one and +1 to two of the player's choice (or +1 to four), not +1 Strength and +1 Dexterity. Aleena speed is 25 (code 35). 20 of the 23 trait names are invented.
- Backgrounds: every background grants a feat at level 1 and a choice of 2 skills from 4. The code grants no feat (FeatsSelection allows floor(level / 4), so 0 at level 1) and never applies background skills. Ace Pilot, Diplomat, Outsider and the picker's Scholar, Pilot, Merchant are not on the site.
- Feats: 4 of the 13 in feats.ts exist (Alert, Force-Sensitive, Linguist, Tough) and 1 of the 5 the picker shows.
- Powers: 9 of 13 force powers exist by name and only 2 have the right level (Force Storm is 9th level, code 5th). None of the 11 tech powers exists.
- Equipment (equipment.ts, items.ts): 14 of 26 and 13 of 27 names exist; one price in each file matches. Blaster pistol is 175 cr (code 450 and 500), lightsaber 450 cr and 1d6 (code 3000 and 2d8), vibroblade 1d8 (code 1d4). Starting credits are a flat 500; the site uses class starting equipment or a roll such as 8d4 x 100 cr for a Fighter.
- Alignment: site is Light, Dark or Balanced combined with Lawful, Chaotic or Neutral. The code's five options are not SW5e.
- Combat in Play: a natural 1 does not auto-miss; the attack bonus is modifier + 2 at every level; damage is d8 melee and d6 ranged whatever weapon is carried; 0 HP is "defeated" with no death saves; Rest is a free long rest. Test Combat's Stormtrooper (30 HP, AC 13, +3) and Imperial Officer (20 HP, AC 12, +2) are not the site's Trooper (13 HP, AC 15, +5, 1d8+3) and Junior Officer (13 HP, AC 12, +3, 1d6+1), each CR 1/2.

Present on the site, absent or token in code: 137 archetypes (code 13, three real names, invented features), 119 maneuvers (2, neither real), 32 fighting styles and 32 masteries (1 real name), 20 lightsaber forms (0), 46 weapon and 30 armor properties (2), 1918 enhanced items (0), 271 monsters (15 entries, none with the site's numbers), Exhaustion condition, multiclassing. Starships: the site builds a ship from size (Tiny to Gargantuan), tier, hull and shield dice, six crew deployments, 257 modifications, 104 equipment items and 67 ventures; the code has five fixed stat blocks with sizes like "Colossal" that the site does not use.

Site pages with no counterpart: Tools is only a login-gated Character Creator (steps Species, Class, Ability Scores, Description, Equipment; Standard Array, Point Buy or Manual, same three methods as here). Assets is PDFs of the four rulebooks, character, deployment and starship sheets, expanded-content links, a podcast.

Done on 2026-10-13: the data files are generated from the API, expanded content included (the user asked for all of it).

## Decisions
- Package is ESM.
- Storage: Postgres on this VM with node-postgres (user accepted the default).
- Deleted CharacterCreationForm.tsx (dead, corrupted, unreferenced); pages/CharacterCreation.tsx is the creation flow.
- No 2D maps (user, 2026-10-13): what is where is shown on the 3D map and used by tapping it. Lists and buttons in the HUD stay, as another way to do the same things.
- The server binds loopback by default (2c). Phase 3 decides what sits in front of it.
- The class picker offers only classes that have data (2c). Revisit when data for the other three is added.
- Playwright is a dev dependency, pinned (2c).
- Rules data is generated, never hand-written (2d). All ten classes are offered again now that all have data.
- New campaigns always get a generated world; campaigns without one are offered one in Play (changed 2026-10-13; they kept a plain location list before).
- The generator's code lives in the existing shared/logic module and the editor in the existing CampaignManager, at the user's request to extend files rather than add them. The only new files are the import script and the generated data.
- Access is Tailscale-only (user, 2026-10-13); public HTTPS stays a possible later development. No Oracle port is opened for the game.
- Phase 2f added no source files: `lib/sw5e/characterCalculation.ts` (an unused helper with two hard-coded experience tables) was rewritten as the module for features and levelling. New files are `deploy/*` and migration 0001.
- Rules the books leave open are settled by the labelled house rules under "Play rules". Starting equipment follows the starting wealth variant.
