# Terrain Studio for SW5e

**Terrain Studio** (HowdyMoto/terrain-tools, MIT, 2026; <https://github.com/HowdyMoto/terrain-tools>) is a Three.js terrain authoring toolkit and local browser editor: sculpt brushes, hydraulic and thermal erosion with preview, four-layer PBR surface rules by altitude and slope, foliage brushes, water, PNG16/RAW16 heightmap exchange, first-person walk-through. It needs Node 22.12+ and Chrome or Edge with WebGPU (no WebGL fallback). It was cloned on 2026-11-10 to a scratch folder (not in this repo; 205 MB with its assets). Its code is not a dependency of the game: it edits one landscape at a time, by hand, in WebGPU. **What the game takes from it is its method**, and its erosion maths (the droplet loop and the conservative thermal step are ported, credited in CREDITS.md), as `shared/logic/terrain.ts`: pure, seeded, nothing stored but the spec.

Use the editor when you want to *look* at a landform and learn what a setting does: `git clone https://github.com/HowdyMoto/terrain-tools.git <scratch>/terrain-tools`, `npm ci`, `npm run dev`, open the printed URL, **Sculpt -> Terrain -> Generate** (Alpine, Dunes, Islands), **Weather landscape** (hydraulic, thermal or both; Original/Result toggle), **Heightmap exchange** (export a 16-bit PNG to compare against our `terrainFor` output). Clone into its own new folder, run `npm ci` there, never from inside a folder of ours.

## The order (do not merge passes, do not drop one)

`makeTerrain(spec)` runs these in this order. Each undoes something the one before did.

1. **Sculpt.** Warped, slope-damped fractal noise for the base (Quilez: each octave is damped by the slope the coarser ones made, so steep ground is smooth and flat ground gets detail); ridged multifractal for mountain ranges (each octave weighted by the one before: rough peaks, smooth valleys), mixed in only where a low-frequency mask allows; `pow(h, power)` so valleys are flat and tops sharp; dunes (long windward side, short slip face), terraces (mesas), craters (rim and bowl). **Domain-warp every lookup** or the ridges align with the grid. Rotate and shift every octave. Normalise to 0..relief only after mixing.
2. **Stream power** (Braun and Willett 2013). Receiver = steepest of 8 neighbours; sort high to low, accumulate drainage area; incise low to high so the receiver already has its new height: `F = k * min(A, cap)^m / dist; h = (h + F * h_recv) / (1 + F)`. This is what makes valleys *branch*. Droplets alone only scratch slopes. `k` 0.12 and `m` 0.5 behave; bigger stays stable and still looks wrong (knife ridges). Cap the area or one basin carves a canyon.
3. **Droplets** (Lague; Beyer; Terrain Studio's `erodeTerrain`). Inertia 0.15, capacity 4, erode 0.3, deposit 0.3, evaporation 0.04, radius 2, up to 48 steps. **Gate the erosion by slope** (`smoothstep(tan 18 deg, tan 30 deg)`): flats that get scratched look like noise again. Spread the take over a small radial kernel (single-cell trenches read as scratches). Keep the outer ring fixed.
4. **Thermal.** Past the angle of repose (talus 28-38 degrees) the excess slides to the lower neighbour, an eighth to each at most, so no ground is lost and nothing overshoots.
5. **Polish.** Blur the flat ground, weighted by flatness, with a radius that varies by place. A fixed radius makes soft ground look like a filter.
6. **Level** where something is built: `levelDisc` (a pad: flat to `r`, blended back over `blend`) and `levelRoad` (the road follows the contour: the heights along it are smoothed over about 260 m and the ground within half the width is brought to that). **Any pass that cuts must be followed by "put the wash back"**: the flat the player or a vehicle uses is applied after the erosion, never before it.
7. **Colour** from where the water went and how steep the ground is (`terrainSplat`, `terrainColorAt`): rock on steep, convex, high ground; sand on flats and in concave, wet places; snow above the biome's line, less on steep ground; soil greener low and drier high; wet where the flow is big and the slope small; hollows darkened (ambient occlusion from the curve). Never paint a slope mask that ignores where the water went.
8. **Water** (`carveBasins`, `terrainWater`): a few bowls are dug on the flattest, wettest ground (noise-made land has few closed hollows), then the water is the *priority-flood fill* (Barnes 2014: each hollow stands to the lowest point of its rim); keep only the broadest few that are not too deep (`TerrainStyle.lakes`, none on a dry world), the rest are dry hollows. Rivers are drainage areas gathered over the *filled* surface (not the stream pass's own, made before the later passes changed the ground), cells past a flow line followed downstream to the edge or a lake, smoothed, as wide as the river is big. Trees and rocks are never planted in water.
9. **Dress** (`scatterOn`): a jittered grid thinned by density, kept where slope, height and flow allow. Trees on gentle ground below the tree line, rocks on the in-between slopes. Never noise thresholds for placement (they clump in rows).

## Realism rules (the expert list)

- Real ground is about 1/f: if a band of frequency is loud for its wavelength the land looks cartoony. Gain 0.5 per octave is the floor; damped fbm lowers the loud bands further.
- A map needs flat places and sharp places. One exponent (`power` 1.2 to 2.4) does it. Hills everywhere read as a pillow.
- Gullies, rills and rivers go downhill. Isotropic noise on a slope reads as static.
- Rivers first, land second, where you can (Bremen CGI 2022): fill depressions, route D8, count the area, call large areas rivers and filled pits lakes. Rivers carved after the land run uphill at the joins; carve with the same height function the vehicles and the mesh use (`groundAt`).
- Scale sanity (1 square = 1.4 m, `UNITS_PER_M` 0.711): a hover race wants slope under about 1 in 10 where it runs; a settlement wants a level pad; a hillside a fighter flies along wants 1 in 3 to 1 in 1; real talus is 30-38 degrees; a mountain range at a settlement's horizon is 100-400 m up, 2-10 km off.
- `Math.pow` of a negative is NaN and nothing throws: the mesh, the colours and the camera go to NaN and the page is a sky. Clamp every `pow`.
- One basin must not drain the map: cap the drainage area.
- Skirts: ease the rim of a finite map to a flat level (`rim`), so the plain that goes on to the horizon joins it; a visible frame means the rim is too wide or too steep.
- Everything seeded and integer-hashed (no `Math.sin` in the noise): the server and every browser must agree to the metre, because ground vessels, bombs and speeders are placed on it.
- Heavy passes are cached (`terrainFor`, 6 kept). Budget: n=129 about 0.3 s, n=257 "light" about 0.4 s, "full" 1.6 s. Pick the grid by use, not by habit; tests start dozens of courses.

## Tuning recipes

- Flatter, softer land: lower `relief`, raise `power`, raise `polish` passes. Craggier: lower `power` toward 1, raise `ridges` to 0.6, lower `warp`.
- A mesa country: `terrace` 4-6, `ridges` 0.2, thermal high (the steps keep their cliffs only if the talus is steep).
- Dunes: `dunes` 0.4-0.6, `sandy` 0.7, droplets 0.3, thermal 14. Keep `power` near 1.2.
- A river valley with a walkable floor: sculpt a path, stream power, then `levelRoad` along the path (the wash back), then droplets (gated), then polish.
- Cliffs that overhang cannot be stored in a height field; fold vertices on slopes past about 45 degrees downhill in XZ for the camera (Vault 80's "fold"), keep it small, it reads from the path only. Not done in the game.
- More detail at no cost: sample the field with extra octaves in the colour and the normal, not in the mesh (the mesh only smooths what the field has).

## Pro tips from Terrain Studio's docs that the game does not use yet (read 2026-11-11: MATERIALS, FOLIAGE, ENVIRONMENT)

- **Four layers, ruled by altitude (m) and slope (degrees), with feathering; paint overrides only where art needs it.** Where two layers meet, the *height map of each layer* decides which wins (rock shows through the cracks of soil), not a straight cross-fade. Ours: `terrainSplat` has the weights, but the ground is one photograph with the colour on top.
- **Triplanar on slopes** (no UVs, no stretch). A 26 m gorge wall on a planar XZ map is a smear. Use it for the rock layer only (or two projections, "biplanar").
- **Reduce texture repetition** = three randomly offset patches blended with fourth-power triangular weights, per layer, for soil, grass, rock and sand; never for roads, pavers or anything with a pattern. Ours tiles every 8 squares (11 m): the repeat is the first thing the eye finds.
- **Texture scale in metres, per layer**, shared by that layer's maps; a layer's maps are one array so a shift found in the height lines up on the others. Pack AO, roughness and metalness in one map (R, G, B): fewer fetches.
- **Light the scene like the photographs**: a low sun (15:00), filtered sky fill, contact occlusion from curvature, 8x anisotropy on the ground, and *no light baked into albedo* (keep directional shading out of the colour). A flat Lambert ground with a vertex colour looks like a model; the same ground with a low sun and fog reads as land.
- **Foliage brush = a mixture** of entries with weights (3:1 is 75:25), a scale range (start 0.85-1.15), random yaw, *align to surface* for ground cover and upright for trees, a wind bend mask by height (stiff below 0.55 of the height), and a **spacing that is the minimum distance between every entry of the brush** (brushes may overlap each other). Placement is **limited to material weights** (grass only where the soil layer is strong), so the splat and the scatter agree. Distance: up to three LODs, retained density falling with distance for ground cover (not for a tree line), a cull distance, a dithered cross-fade, impostors for far trees. Cap the instances (the editor's is 50 000; a phone's is far less).
- **Scatter is seeded** (same seed, same positions): ours is too (`scatterOn`), and must stay so for the server.

## Placement rules from an ecology, not from noise (our design, after Star Citizen's Planet Tech)

What is public about Genesis / Planet Tech v4-v5 is thin (read 2026-11-11: the wiki, CitizenCon 2019 "Terra Firmer" summary, 2026 explainers; no CIG talk gives thresholds): **temperature and humidity maps choose the biome** (two climate layers replaced baked colour textures, so the surface follows the climate); v4 added terrain texture blending, object scattering and *biome transitions*; v5 adds geology, soil type, soil depth and nutrients, and biomes that *emerge from each plant's requirements*; loose rock collects under cliffs and along slopes; cliffs use triplanar mapping. No slope or altitude numbers are published: **the numbers below are this game's own house rules**, to be tuned by eye.

One table, `ECOLOGY[biome]`, should feed the ground colour, the scatter and the settlement assets (a farmland's crops use it too). Each entry says what it *needs*; the placement is what is left:

| Need | Taken from | Rule of thumb (house, untuned) |
|---|---|---|
| slope | `terrainSlope` | trees < 0.35 (about 19 degrees), shrubs < 0.55, rocks any, scree 0.25-0.8 |
| soil depth | curvature and slope: flat or concave deep, convex and steep thin | trees need deep, shrubs thin, rock needs none |
| wetness | `terrainFlow` plus concavity plus distance to `terrainWater` | reeds in the shallows, then grass, shrubs, trees behind (Mixar's `riparian` kit orders it the same way: water, riprap, grassy banks, tree line) |
| altitude band | height over the terrain's own min..max | tree line at the style's `snow` share minus a margin; scrub above it |
| aspect | the world's sun azimuth | dry, bare slopes face the sun; moss and ferns on the shaded side (a cheap, strong cue) |
| competition | spacing between entries, a cluster seed | clumps and clearings: a cluster mask (low-frequency noise through a threshold), not uniform spacing |
| clearance | settlement pads, `roads`, water | none on a pad or in a lane or in water; density rises with distance from a road and falls toward a pad |

Emergent behaviours worth having (each is one rule, not hand placement): a **talus apron** (rocks where steep ground gives way to a concave foot, density by the steepness above, sunk 20-40 % into the ground, half-aligned to the normal); a **wadi** in a desert (the only shrubs sit where flow is high and slope low); a **riparian band** along a river; a **verge** along a road (berm, gravel, tufts, a culvert where the road crosses a drainage line); **ecotones** (between two biomes' entries the density of each fades over a distance, so a place's edge is not a line); a **settlement edge** (clutter, ruts, fences, field walls rising toward the pad). Three scatter tiers by distance: *cover* (grass, pebbles: shader or instanced, near only), *mid* (shrubs, rocks), *tall* (trees, boulders: far, with an impostor). Do not align a tree to the slope, do align a rock halfway; always bury a rock.

| Tool | Does | Does not |
|---|---|---|

| **Terrain Studio editor** (scratch clone, WebGPU) | look-dev of a style by eye; 16-bit heightmap export to compare | run in the game |

| **Mixar** (open scene tabs, MCP) | the same dump in a *new scene tab* for review (a 65 x 65 height field as base64 fits the 32 KB script limit; `render_viewport`); `list_terrain_assets` (52 scanned plants and rocks, grouped by biome, free) as a shopping list and a shape reference; `import_terrain_asset` in a scratch tab | spending credits unasked (the account had 0 on 2026-11-11; AI-generated meshes are allowed since the ban was lifted, quote the job first); shipping the library's assets before their licence is known (record it in CREDITS.md first, or build our own); touching the user's open tabs |

How to look at a place: `npx tsx .claude/skills/terrain-studio/preview/dump.mts <biomes> <outdir>` writes `<biome>.h.bin` (float32 heights), `.c.bin` (the splat colours) and `.json` (road, trees, rocks); `blender -b --factory-startup --python-exit-code 1 -P .claude/skills/terrain-studio/preview/shade.py -- <biome> <0|1 detail> <out.png> <outdir>` renders it in Cycles in about 15 s (a 600 m patch at 3 m cells). Results of the first run: the loop works; forms read far better under a low sun than as a shaded-relief PNG; **noise colour and bump on top of a vertex colour is a small gain** (a faint grain), so the aesthetic win is not noise alone but *structure*: slope- and flow-driven layers, ruts and berms on roads, a talus at the cliff foot; and the mesh at 3 m cells cannot hold anything under about 6 m, so everything finer must come from the fragment shader (normal, height, detail noise), not from vertices. The proxy rocks I scattered stuck out of slopes like thorns: rocks must be sunk and half-aligned.

## Built 2026-11-11 (commits d0a199a, e172f60, b710873, f869ddc)

The game draws through a **Babylon.js mirror of the three scene** (`babylonGl`), so a three `onBeforeCompile` shader does nothing: a surface effect is a `ShaderPatch` kind in `babylon.ts` (a `CodePlugin` into Babylon's PBR hooks: `CUSTOM_FRAGMENT_UPDATE_ALBEDO`, `_BEFORE_LIGHTS` for the normal, `_UPDATE_ALPHA`). **Do not redeclare the uniforms listed in the plugin's `ubo`** (Babylon declares them: a "redefinition" error and a washed-out scene); extra vertex attributes pass through by name (`attributes: [...]` in the plugin). `/dev/terrain` now uses `babylonGl` (`three=1` for three's own), with `view=ground` (eye height) and `fog=0`.

- **Ground material v2** (`kind: "ground"`, `GroundLayers`): the hue is the vertex colour (the palette), the photographs lend pattern and relief only (the picture divided by its mean colour); four layers (soil, rock, pale, wet) by the vertex attribute `splat` (`terrainSurface` gives colour and weights in one call), height-blended, two octaves of world noise, a second turned tap against the repeat, the rock mapped along three axes with its normal map, normals faded out past 90-260 squares; lite tier = soil and rock, one tap, no normals. Used by `ReliefGround`, `RaceGround` and `GroundSurface` (the walkable plane, with its offset parallax kept).
- **Ways**: `raceWay` (the signed side of the centre line) -> the attribute `way`: two wheel ruts, a berm, a compacted pale middle, no ribbon.
- **Water** (`kind: "water"`): depth tint from the vertex attribute `shore` (metres; negative on a dry corner, so the shader cuts the shore where the ground rises through the water), foam in the shallows, two drifting sets of ripples lighting it; rivers have three vertices across, so the middle is deep.
- **Kit** (`tools/blender/build-terrain-kit.py` -> `client/public/figures/terrain-kit.glb`, 70 KB): `boulder_a/b/c` (320 triangles), `scree` (220), `ledge_a/b` (150: an overhang with its back sunk in the slope), `mouth` (a cave mouth, 1300: built, **not yet placed**: it is for the caves card). Placed by `kitSpots` (pure, tested): boulders off the flat, scree at the foot of a slope, ledges on cliffs past 0.85, turned downhill. Vertex colours are the rock's tone in light (about 0.2) times its occlusion; the material colour is the biome's rock colour times 4.2.
- **Not yet**: puddles on streets, steep parallax, the street ground with slopes (needs y != 0 everywhere), kit pieces for farms, the `ECOLOGY` table (the rules above are prose; only `kitSpots` is code).

## Surface plan (design; the stages named in "Built" are done, the rest is not)

Today: `GroundSurface` is one Poly Haven set on a flat plane (8 squares a tile, one-tap offset parallax at 0.045: it swam above that, 2026-11-01); `ReliefGround` and `RaceGround` are Lambert vertex colour with no texture; every shader change must be mirrored for the Babylon renderer (`shaderPatch` in `babylon.ts`). Stages, each a small commit, each checked on the lite tier first:

1. **Ground material v2** (the biggest win for the least cost): the splat weights (rock, soil, sand, wet) ride a second vertex attribute; 3-4 layers (albedo + height in one image, normal + AO in another, 512 px, one array) blended by weight with height-blend `max(h_layer + w)`; **macro noise** (two world-space value-noise octaves multiplying the albedo: no fetch) and a **second tap at a rotated 0.37x scale** against the repeat; a branch skips layers under 2 % weight; lite tier = 2 layers, no second tap. Roughness from the ORM. Same for `ReliefGround`, `RaceGround` and the street ground.
2. **Triplanar for the rock layer** where slope passes about 35 degrees (gorge walls, cliffs, cave mouths).
3. **Roads and tracks as a layer**: a distance-to-polyline weight from the spec's `roads` (compacted, a shade off the soil, *no markings*, a feathered edge), two wheel ruts as a normal-map stripe along the road's tangent (UV along the line from a ribbon mesh a hand above the ground, alpha-feathered), the berm already in the height (`levelRoad` shoulders), gravel and tufts by the verge rule. Puddles on streets: roughness 0.1 and a darker albedo where the ground is concave, flat and the world is wet.
4. **Parallax**: keep the one-tap offset (cheap; leave it at 0.045); steep-parallax (8-16 steps) only on the road ribbon and bare rock near the camera and only off the lite tier; for silhouettes use real geometry (a finer ground mesh near the camera: 1.4 m cells on the street map), not parallax.
5. **Water**: lakes and rivers get a shader instead of a translucent quad: depth tint from (water level minus ground), a foam line where depth < 0.3 m, two scrolling normal maps, a Fresnel sky colour (through the fog colour), no refraction; river ribbons scroll along their length; the ground's `wet` weight darkens the shore. Reeds and lotus in the shallows from the ecology table.
6. **Kit pieces from Blender** (original, CC0, instanced by chunk with culling): cliff blocks and rim caps, **overhang and ledge pieces** snapped to steep cells (this is how a height field gets overhangs), cave mouth portals, boulder groups, scree, dead trees and stumps, shrubs, reeds, tufts, a ford with stepping stones, a plank footbridge, a culvert, field walls and fences (farmlands), a vaporator and a pit-farm dome (the hollow homestead). Rocks and trees carry an LOD or an impostor.
7. **The walkable street ground itself** (slopes, graded roads): needs every figure, building and the camera off y = 0: its own card.

Costs to watch on a phone: texture fetches per pixel (4 layers x 2 images = 8 at worst; the branch and the lite tier cut it to 2-4), the number of instances (cull by chunk, thin cover with distance), and overdraw on water. Measure on the lite tier before the full one.

## How to check a change

1. `npx tsx --test tests/world/terrain.test.ts tests/ships/airfight.test.ts tests/world/skies.test.ts tests/world/roadtrip.test.ts tests/world/convoy.test.ts`; then `npx tsc --noEmit` and a scratch `vite build` (no warnings).
2. Look at it without a campaign: `/dev/terrain?biome=forest&seed=5&view=high|low|top&inner=120` (the relief round a flat place), `/dev/dogfight?scenario=defend|raid|escort|strike&biome=desert&seed=9`, `/dev/dogfight?game=race&biome=jungle&seed=3`. Dev server on a test port only (see CLAUDE.md), never the production port.
3. For the *shape* of a landform (not the colours) a shaded-relief PNG from a node script is faster than any scene: sample `terrainHeight`, light by the gradient, write a PNG (a 40-line script with `zlib`); judge the drainage, the flats and the rim there. Do this before tuning a style. For the *look* (light, scale, how a place reads from the ground), use the Cycles preview above (`preview/dump.mts`, `preview/shade.py`).
4. Compare with the Terrain Studio editor's own output for the same style when something looks wrong: if its Alpine at the same relief looks better, the difference is usually a missing pass (stream power, the slope gate, polish).
